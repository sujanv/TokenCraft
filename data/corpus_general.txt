Tokenization is the foundational process in Natural Language Processing and modern Large Language Models.
It converts unstructured raw text strings into discrete integer sequences suitable for neural network embeddings.
Traditional rule-based systems relied on whitespace splitting, but suffered from massive vocabularies and out-of-vocabulary failures.
Subword tokenization bridges the gap between whole-word vocabularies and character-level models.
Byte-Pair Encoding, originally developed as a data compression algorithm by Philip Gage in 1994, was adapted for NLP by Rico Sennrich in 2015.
BPE iteratively replaces the most frequent pairs of adjacent bytes or characters in a corpus with a single, unused symbol.
OpenAI adopted byte-level BPE for GPT-2, GPT-3, GPT-4, and the Tiktoken library, ensuring 100% vocabulary coverage with zero unknown tokens.
WordPiece, introduced by Schuster and Nakajima in 2012 and popularized by Google BERT, uses likelihood ratio scoring rather than raw frequencies.
Continuation subwords in WordPiece are denoted using prefix markers such as double hashes.
Character-level tokenization operates directly on Unicode codepoints, maintaining tiny vocabularies at the cost of high sequence lengths.
Fertility measures the average number of subword tokens produced per word in natural language text.
Compression ratio measures the average number of raw UTF-8 bytes represented per token.
Properly handling punctuation, contractions, casing, numbers, and special symbols is critical for tokenizer performance.
High-quality tokenizers preserve semantic boundaries and enable efficient transformer attention across large context windows.
TokenCraft provides an educational, production-ready environment to study and visualize these algorithms side-by-side.
